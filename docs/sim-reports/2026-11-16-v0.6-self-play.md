# Self-play of the v0.6 prototype: does the game now need thought?

Run on 16 November 2026 on `prototype/` with `config/economy.json` preset `v0.6`. It follows the
[first self-play report](2026-11-16-prototype-self-play.md), which found that the v0.4 prototype could
be won on one fixed build order. The rule changes are listed in the
[changelog](../design/CHANGELOG.md) and explained in [decision 0008](../decisions/0008-rules-that-reward-thought.md).

## What was played

| How | Matches |
| --- | --- |
| A player in seat 0 with one of 14 fixed build orders, against the new bots, at 2, 3 and 4 players, 150 matches each | 6,300 |
| The same seat played by the bots' own planner, with and without looking ahead, and by a one-round lookahead | 1,350 |
| The same seat played by a player that thinks every option through to the election, 40 matches each | 120 |
| All-bot tables | 1,350 |
| One match through the real page in a phone-size browser, four players, all 18 rounds | 1 |

Reproduce with `node prototype/test/selfplay.js 150 --deep=40`. With 150 matches a win rate is good to
about 4 points either way; the 40-match rows to about 7.

The rivals are stronger than in the first report. Each bot now drafts a plan in five styles, plays
every draft forward to round 18 and locks the one that ends furthest ahead. So "before" and "after"
differ in rules and in opposition, which is the change a player will meet.

## Headline

1. **Rote play no longer wins.** The best fixed build order wins 28%, 31% and 39% at 2, 3 and 4
   players (even chance 50 / 33 / 25). Under v0.4 it was 55%, 39% and 54%. Eleven of the fourteen
   orders are now below an even chance at every player count.
2. **Thinking further ahead wins more.** Against the same rivals, at four players: a fixed order 0%
   to 39%, the bots' planner without lookahead 13%, with lookahead 25%, a one-round lookahead 33%, and
   a player that compares many plans through to the election 75%.
3. **Finishes are closer and decided later** at three and four players.
4. **Coins no longer decide the election.** Campaign votes fell from 53% to 64% of a seat's
   votes to 16% to 18%.
5. **Three problems remain**: two fixed investing orders still beat an even chance at four players,
   timber is the best start at four players, and two-player matches are still decided early.

Reading it:

- **Anything that only sells loses outright.** With prices that slide and works that cost upkeep, a
  seat that floods its own market earns little. Under v0.4 "build raw works and sell" won 46% at two
  players.
- **The old winning line is ordinary now.** "Goods works second, and invest" won 54% at four players
  under v0.4 and wins 25% here.
- **Looking ahead is worth a great deal.** The bots' planner alone wins 13% to 15%; the same planner
  choosing between its drafts by playing them out wins an even share. The last row does the same
  with more drafts and wins three matches in four. A person who plans should expect to sit between
  those two rows, and a person who does not should expect to lose.
- **Two fixed orders still do too well at four players**: the goods works with one bond (39%) and
  investing only (37%). Both rush shares. See "What is still wrong".

## What the tuning runs found on the way

- **A seat with no coins could not sell.** Its route crossed a rival link, the toll had to be paid in
  advance, and the trade was refused every round to the end of the match. This was in v0.4 too. Tolls
  on a sale now come out of the proceeds.
- **The starting works is the most fragile part of the balance.** Small changes to founding goods or
  to how the bots judged brick swung brick starts between 5% and 66%. Round 0 of the rules (a sealed
  bid for pick order), which the prototype does not have, is the proper answer.
- **A bonus for the largest shareholder of a district** made margins wider, and **larger projects**
  made shares half of all votes without closer finishes. Neither was kept.
- **The page stays quick.** The slowest lock in the browser match, with three thinking bots, took
  about a quarter of a second on this machine.

## What is still wrong

1. **A share rush at four players.** The five projects hold about 31 shares in all and a share is
   worth 1 to 3 votes, against 25 coins for a campaign vote. A seat that buys goods and invests them
   every round, without reading the board, wins 37% to 39%. The bots do not race for shares hard
   enough to stop it. Either shares are too cheap, or imports should not be investable.
2. **Timber is the best start at four players (42%) and food the worst (15%).** Timber feeds both
   recipes and two of the three founding lists.
3. **Two players is still decided early** (the winner leads for good from round 9, margin 25%).
4. **The consumer goods stall is still empty in 57% to 73% of rounds**, against a target of a quarter.
5. **The evidence is bots against bots.** It shows that fixed play loses and that lookahead wins; it
   cannot show how the game feels. A few matches by people are the next test.
6. **The economy simulator does not play v0.6.** These numbers come from the prototype's engine only.
