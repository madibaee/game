# Self-play of the v0.6 prototype, second round

Run on 16 November 2026 on `prototype/` with `config/economy.json` preset `v0.6`. Nothing in the rules,
the numbers or the bots was changed since the [first v0.6 report](2026-11-16-v0.6-self-play.md). This
round asks two things: do the first report's numbers hold on maps no earlier run has seen, and what
does the game feel like when every action is chosen by hand.

## What was played

| How | Matches |
| --- | --- |
| A player in seat 0 with one of 14 fixed build orders, against the thinking bots, at 2, 3 and 4 players, 150 matches each, all on new maps | 6,300 |
| The same seat played by the bots' own planner, with and without looking ahead, and by a one-round lookahead | 1,350 |
| The same seat played by a player that thinks every option through to the election, 40 matches each | 120 |
| All-bot tables | 1,350 |
| Matches played by hand, every action chosen from the board state, against the thinking bots | 2 |

Reproduce the scripted rows with `node prototype/test/selfplay.js 150 --deep=40 --seed=1000000`. The
new `--seed` flag shifts every map seed. With 150 matches a win rate is good to about 4 points either
way, and the 40-match rows to about 7.

## Headline

1. **The first report's numbers hold on new maps.** Every figure in the tables below is within the
   noise of the first batch, with one exception in point 2.
2. **Investing without building is now the best fixed order at three and four players** (35% and
   33%, against an even chance of 33% and 25%). In the first batch it was 26% and 37%. Shares are
   still too cheap for what they score.
3. **Both hand-played matches were won, by 41 to 24 and by 45 to 36, and both on shares.** In the
   second match the runner-up finished with 240 coins and 15 building votes against 77 coins and 6,
   and still lost by 9 because 17 shares scored 36 votes.
4. **Keeping a district served is the one hard decision the game now has, and it is a good one.** It
   cost 8 votes when misjudged in the first match and decided the second.
5. **The building game ends early.** All five projects were filled by round 7 and round 9. After that
   a seat with money has nothing to spend it on.
6. **No engine fault.** No negative balance and no anomaly in 9,120 scripted matches or in the two
   hand-played ones.

## The hand-played matches

### Match 1: four players, seed 2026, food start. Won 41 to 24, 15 and 12.

- **Rounds 1 to 8, the land grab.** One bond in round 1, then goods bought in the market went
  straight into the commercial project next to the start (all 7 shares) and a residential one (4 of
  5). A second bond in round 6 paid for a goods works. All five projects were full by round 7 and the
  last free site went in round 8.
- **Rounds 9 to 13, nothing left to claim.** Both bonds repaid, all three works raised to tier III.
- **Rounds 13 to 18, the serving puzzle.** The city serves districts oldest first. The two starting
  districts stand ahead of every founded one, so six goods a round against a demand of eight left the
  player's own commercial district unserved. Holding four goods back one round and selling ten the
  next kept it served every second round, which is enough to avoid decline.
- **The mistake.** In round 15 the hand sold enough for the district to rise to class III three
  rounds before the election. A class III commercial district needs a tool as well, the only tool in
  the stall went to the older district, and four timber were thrown away over the storage cap, which
  cut the next round's goods from six to four. Both of the player's districts fell a class in round
  17. The score went from 47 to 39 in one round.
- **Final:** 18 share votes, 9 for works, 14 for 362 coins. Two of the three bots ended with under 25
  coins and few shares.

### Match 2: three players, seed 777, iron start. Won 45 to 36 and 29.

- **A bad opening.** Three plans lost items to input mistakes: the whole of round 1, a build in round
  4 and a sale in round 9. In round 9 the seat had no coins, so both works stood idle for want of
  upkeep.
- **Shares anyway.** Iron and bought brick, timber and food went into four projects: 17 shares by
  round 9 (5 of 6, 6 of 6, 1 of 6, 5 of 7) for goods worth about 100 coins.
- **Level at 37 votes each going into round 17.** The rival had five tier III works and 185 coins.
  The player had two works and 62 coins.
- **What decided it.** Four tools, two imported and two bought from the stall (51 coins), were held and three sold in
  round 17 so that the player's six-share industrial district stayed at class III. The same round the rival's
  residential district fell a class because no goods reached the stall, and the player was the only
  maker of goods at the table. Three late upgrades for 48 coins added three votes.
- **Final:** 36 share votes, 6 for works, 3 for 77 coins, against 12, 15 and 9 for 240 coins.

## What the hand play shows

Working as intended:

1. **Timing sales now matters.** When to hold stock, when to sell, and when to let a district rise are
   real decisions, and a wrong one is punished within two rounds.
2. **Imports are worth their price at the right moment.** Tools at 16 coins each look poor, and they
   made sure of 6 votes in match 2.
3. **Money alone no longer wins.** 240 coins lost to 77.

Still wrong:

1. **Shares are underpriced.** One good worth 4 to 7 coins buys a share worth 1 to 3 votes. A vote
   from coins costs 25. Both hand matches and the "invest only" row say the same thing.
2. **The share race ends by round 7 to 9, and the land runs out by round 8 to 15.** The second half of
   the match has no new projects and no sites. In match 1 the player sat on 180 to 360 coins for five
   rounds with nothing to buy.
3. **The oldest-first serving order favours the two starting districts, which nobody owns.** They take
   the scarce goods and tools ahead of every district a player has paid for. Rising to class III is
   often a loss because of it.
4. **The bots do not defend their districts.** No bot was seen holding stock to keep a district
   served, and none made consumer goods in either match. A human can let a rival's district fall by
   not selling.
5. **Food and goods are the bottleneck, brick, iron and timber are worthless late.** The three raw
   stalls sat full at a price of 1 from round 11 in match 1, while the goods stall was empty in most
   rounds of both matches.
6. **Poorer seats cannot come back.** Two bots in match 1 ended with 12 and 22 coins and never had the
   coins to upgrade or import.

Rough edges met while playing:

1. One wrong line in a plan drops that action without warning at lock, and a dropped sale can cost a
   whole round. The page guards against most of it. Plans sent straight to the engine are not guarded.
2. An investment that is returned because the project filled in the same round still costs the toll.
3. A plan cannot buy and sell the same good in one round, so imported goods can only be sold a round
   later. That is reasonable, and the page should say it.
4. A seat with no coins cannot pay upkeep and its works stand idle. There is no warning the round
   before.
5. Goods over the storage cap are thrown away in a fixed order that the player cannot choose. In match
   1 it took the timber the goods works needed.

## Suggested next steps

1. Make shares cost more as a project fills, or give fewer shares for cheap goods, so that "invest
   only" drops below an even chance.
2. Keep something to claim in the second half: later project waves, or a second ring of sites that
   opens around round 9.
3. Serve founded districts before the starting ones, or let the owners' own deliveries count first.
4. Teach the bots to protect a district they hold shares in, and to build a goods works when the goods
   stall has been empty for several rounds.
5. Let the player choose what to discard over the storage cap.
